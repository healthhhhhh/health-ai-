## Who can use HealthMate

- **13 and older, in the United States.**
- **Not under 13.** If you tell us you're under 13 when you sign up, we don't create an account and
  don't keep what you typed. If we learn later that an account belongs to someone under 13, we lock it
  right away (nothing more is collected or sent to the AI) and delete it after
  [[OWNER/COUNSEL: hold period; code default 72 hours]], so a mistyped date can be fixed by contacting
  [[OWNER: support contact]]. Entering a different date after that doesn't unlock the account.

## How we check your age

We ask for your **date of birth** and work out your age group on our servers: 13–15, 16–17 or 18+.
We don't ask for ID, a selfie or anything biometric. Until you give your date of birth, health features
stay locked. Please give your real date of birth — it decides which protections apply to you.

**Your birthdays.** If you're 13–17 we keep the date you'll turn 18, so your age group updates by itself
on your birthdays. We never send it, or your date of birth, to the AI — at most your age in years. If
you later enter a date that makes you older than we have on record, we keep the younger age group.

## Your privacy as a teen

- **Your account is yours.** HealthMate has no parent or guardian access. We don't show, send or
  report your conversations, records or photos to your parents, school or anyone else.
  [[COUNSEL: list any legal exceptions that apply to HealthMate — e.g. legal process; confirm whether
  any state requires parental access or notice for a minor's data held by a non-provider app.]]
- **No ads, no selling, no tracking for marketing** — for anyone.
- **You control the AI.** AI features only work if you turn them on, and you can turn them off any time.
- **You can delete** anything, or your whole account, in Settings.

## Sensitive topics

You can ask about things like sexual health, mental health, alcohol or drugs, or your body.
HealthMate's AI Health Assistant gives general information and will suggest talking to a clinician,
school nurse or counselor — and, where it might help, a parent or another adult you trust. Many health
services offer confidential care for young people; the rules depend on your state, so ask the service.

**If you're in danger or thinking about hurting yourself:** call or text **988**, or call **911**.
HealthMate shows emergency guidance for messages like these straight away — without the AI.
